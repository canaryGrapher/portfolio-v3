Before you go all Mike Tyson on me, yes, sorry, this title was little bit of a ragebait.

No, your UPI payments are not gonna bear extra charges. But that doesn't mean that it is going to be free for all. 


What has happened is that the government has introduced a legal framework that would allow Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) to be brought back in the future, and several fee models are currently under discussion.  

Here’s what is happening:

What is MDR?

MDR (Merchant Discount Rate) is a fee charged to the merchant, not directly to the customer, whenever a digital payment is made.

For example:

* You buy groceries worth ₹5,000.
* You pay via UPI.
* If MDR is 0.4%, the merchant receives ₹4,980 and ₹20 goes to the payment ecosystem (banks, payment providers, etc.).

Today, for standard UPI merchant payments, this MDR is effectively 0%, which is why UPI is called “free.”

⸻

Why is the government considering bringing it back?

Banks and payment companies have argued for years that:

* They spend heavily on servers, fraud prevention, customer support, and payment infrastructure.
* UPI transaction volumes have exploded, but there is almost no direct revenue from merchant transactions.
* The government compensates the ecosystem only partially, and providers say that is no longer sustainable.  

⸻

What is being proposed?

No final model has been approved, but the leading proposal is:

* Merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000
* Only for larger businesses (for example, annual turnover above roughly ₹1.5 crore)
* MDR of around 0.3%–0.5%  

Other models are also reportedly being discussed, so these numbers are not final.

⸻

Will customers have to pay?

Officially: No.

MDR is a merchant fee, just like with debit and credit cards.

However, merchants could indirectly recover the cost by:

* increasing prices,
* offering discounts only for cash,
* setting minimum purchase amounts for UPI (where permitted).

So while you may not see a “UPI charge” on your screen, the cost could eventually be reflected in prices.  

⸻

What stays free?

The current discussions indicate that these remain unaffected:

* Person-to-person transfers (sending money to friends or family)
* Small everyday UPI purchases
* Most low-value merchant transactions  

⸻

Has it been approved?

No.

Current status:

* Parliament has been presented with amendments to create the legal basis for charging MDR in the future.
* The government has not announced a final fee structure.
* RBI has acknowledged that payment infrastructure has costs but has also said it is too early to decide on the final implementation.  

What this means for you

Scenario	Status
Sending ₹500 to a friend	No change
Paying ₹800 at a shop	No change expected
Paying ₹5,000 at a large retail chain	Could attract merchant MDR in the future (proposal only)
Paying at a small kirana store	Likely to remain unchanged under current proposals

So, despite the headlines, UPI has not become chargeable today. The government is considering bringing back a limited MDR for certain high-value merchant transactions, while keeping ordinary consumer use and person-to-person transfers effectively free.
